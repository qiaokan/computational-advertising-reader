A reproducible erratum in the original monograph

This section uses the original book's own notation: Z is the competitive price, l>0 its upper bound, and b the bid per request, all three in the same currency / impression; F(b) is the win rate. r is the predicted click-through rate (the probability that each successful impression is clicked), not the monetary value of a click. This differs from the SAM paper’s r in Chapter 2, which denotes reward per conversion; b(r)=ar is a linear bid, and a is a coefficient with monetary units, not the calculator's CPA unit price. T is the total number of bid requests, and B the total media budget; c(b) is the original book's cost notation, whose conditional definition is examined below.

E[·] denotes expectation; E[Z | Z≤b] is the average payment given a win; 1_{Z≤b} is the win indicator, 1 on a win and 0 on a loss. Hence E[Z·1_{Z≤b}] is the per-request average cost that also counts zero spend on losses. Uniform denotes the uniform distribution on the given interval.

The arXiv v2 of “Display Advertising with Real-Time Bidding and Behavioural Targeting” has 122 pages, a version date of July 15, 2017, and a PDF title-page date of July 18. It provides a complete RTB main track, but the uniform-distribution second-price example in Section 5.3.4 mixes conditional and unconditional cost. arXiv metadata

Eq 5.5 on PDF page 54 defines the conditional payment after a win; yet Eq 5.35 on page 58 writes c(b) as an integral not divided by the win rate, and the budget equation on page 59 multiplies by the win rate once more. The distinction here should be:

F(b)=\frac{b}{l},\qquad E[Z\mid Z\le b]=\frac{b}{2},\qquad E[Z\mathbf{1}_{Z\le b}]=\frac{b^2}{2l}.

If Z~Uniform(0,l), predicted click-through rate r~Uniform(0,1), and the two are independent, take b(r)=ar; in the unsaturated region 0≤a≤l (no bid exceeds the price upper bound), the average spend per request is obtained by further averaging over r: ∫₀¹ (ar)²/(2l) dr = a²/(6l), and total expected spend is Ta²/(6l). This is an example of maximizing expected clicks within budget; in the above region clicks increase with a. When the budget binds and B/T≤l/6:

a=\sqrt{\frac{6lB}{T}},\qquad b(r)=r\sqrt{\frac{6lB}{T}}.

If b²/(2l), which is already an unconditional expectation, is multiplied again by the win rate b/l, the erroneous per-request “cost” becomes b³/(2l²). Substituting b(r)=ar and integrating over uniform r, the erroneous budget equation, per request, is a³/(8l²)=B/T, which yields the original text's cube-root coefficient, denoted a_paper (the subscript only marks “as given in the original”):

a_{\mathrm{paper}}=2\left(\frac{l^2 B}{T}\right)^{1/3}.

The true expected cost per request is a²/(6l), and the two can be checked directly by substitution.

Checking with l=1, B/T=0.01, the correct coefficient is about 0.244949, and expected spend is exactly 0.01 per request. The original cube-root formula gives about 0.430887, which, substituted back into the true payment formula, gives 0.030944, about 3.094 times the budget; and the bid does not reach the saturation boundary, so the discrepancy cannot be explained by boundary conditions. The main text uses an independent derivation and does not copy this example. Page with the definition, Page with the cost formula, Page with the budget formula

The shortest reading path after today

To fill in the main track, read Chapters 2, 3, 5, and 7 of the RTB monograph; to understand the probability definitions of industrial prediction, read the Google click prediction paper; to build real interfaces, go straight to OpenRTB and your counterparties' contracts; to judge customer value, read randomized advertising experiments. Review record

On November 11, 2026, a separate Claude Opus 5.5 review was conducted on the Chinese edition, covering all eight chapters, formulas, tables, exercises, and calculator code. The first round found no arithmetic or derivation errors, but pointed out gaps such as the incremental ROAS labeling, the successful-impression condition, symbol scoping, and definition before use, and judged that revisions were needed. The editor revised accordingly, and also used the original text to correct the review's misunderstanding of r: in the original monograph's example it is the predicted click-through rate, not a monetary value.

Source verification and mathematical re-checking were conducted separately: Claude reviewed the provided website content and code without opening external links; the editor checked the original literature, including the original book's click-through-rate definition and the conditional/unconditional payment formulas. Derivations were additionally checked with independent numerical integration, grid search over the budget-feasible region, and recomputation of the worked examples. The review also covered the reachability of definitions in the main text, without relying on the advanced sections that are collapsed by default.

The English edition was reviewed separately on November 12, 2026. Corrections addressed translation at inline boundaries, the pacing multiplier, the derivative variable, Axon data qualifiers, chapter references, and billing units. The revised text passed the second review; minor spacing and unit labels were then clarified. Review reports and corrections are recorded in the repository. The review covers the supplied text and code under the stated assumptions, not external source facts, browser rendering, or real-market validity.
