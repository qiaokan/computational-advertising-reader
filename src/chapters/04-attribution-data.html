Attribution, experiments, and data

The delivery policy determines what training data looks like

Ad data is not a passively collected natural sample. Eligibility filtering determines which ads can compete; retrieval determines which candidates are scored; bidding determines which traffic is bought; display success and privacy mechanisms determine which feedback is visible. No model, however clever, can conjure true conversion labels out of actions never tried.

The value of exploration is to establish support overlap (actions the new policy would choose also have a nonzero chance of being executed under the logging policy) and to learn about unknown regions; the cost is real money and user experience. Logging action probabilities can support offline estimators such as IPS (Inverse Propensity Scoring): weight the outcomes of observed actions by “probability the new policy chooses it / probability the logging policy chose it” to estimate the new policy's performance. This requires that the old policy assigns nonzero probability to actions the new policy might take, that the logged probabilities are trustworthy, and that objectives and observation mechanisms are comparable. Small-probability weights bring high variance; clipping reduces variance but introduces bias. Offline replay is not a machine for creating counterfactuals. Original paper on counterfactual risk minimization

Label sampling also changes absolute probabilities. For a binary label under a fixed feature condition, positives are samples where the target event occurred and negatives where it did not. Let p be the event probability in the population before sampling, p_s the event probability among retained samples, and s (0 < s ≤ 1) the retention probability for negatives. If all positives are retained and negatives are independently retained with the same probability s, then:

p=\frac{s p_s}{1-p_s+s p_s}.

For example, s=0.1, p_s=0.5, the true probability is about 0.0909, not 50%. If training has already been corrected with appropriate inverse-probability weighting, you must not blindly correct again; if sampling also depends on features, the corresponding selection probabilities are needed, and a constant cannot simply be applied.

Delayed labels and time leakage

Clicks usually come before purchases, and purchases before long-term contribution profit. A new impression that has not yet converted is not a permanent negative; waiting for all labels to mature costs the model its freshness. In engineering terms, you should specify observation windows, maturity cohorts, delay models, and backfill strategies, and compare experiments at equal maturity. Related research specifically addresses the conflict between maturity and freshness in streaming CVR training. Delayed feedback and elapsed-time sampling

Each training sample should be able to reconstruct the information available at decision time. Account state after the purchase, just-updated cumulative conversion counts, and future attribution outcomes must not leak into pre-purchase features. Ensuring only that table update times are correct is not enough to ensure a correct point-in-time join; you must distinguish event time, processing time, and feature release versions. A model that improves offline results through leakage will not merely be “slightly worse” online—it will lose the very inputs it relied on to predict.

Attribution allocates credit; experiments identify causation

Attribution rules answer “which channel or touchpoint this conversion is credited to,” for example giving credit to the last qualifying click within an agreed window. The incrementality question compares business outcomes “with this advertising intervention” versus “without it.” Real conversions can be miscredited; accurately credited conversions may also have happened anyway.

Research on advertising experiments shows that rich observational data does not guarantee recovery of effects identified by randomized experiments. This does not mean all observational models are useless, but predictive power cannot substitute for causal identification conditions. Gordon et al., advertising measurement research

Below is a separate, independent experiment example, to avoid mixing this example network's media budget into the advertiser's return-on-spend calculation. Randomly split the same eligible population into a treatment group (the campaign is allowed to deliver) and a control group (the campaign is not allowed to deliver) of 100,000 people each. Within the same maturity window, the two groups have 1,200 and 1,000 purchases respectively. Ad spend is 5,000 dollars, and the attribution system credits 500 purchases to the ad; net revenue per purchase is 50 dollars, with a pre-advertising contribution rate of 40%. Here “net revenue” means sales revenue after deducting refunds and other adjustments, before deducting goods, fulfillment, and other costs; a 40% contribution rate means the share left after deducting these non-advertising variable costs. iROAS (incremental ROAS) means true incremental revenue divided by ad spend. Assume randomization and event definitions are correct, the window has matured, and interference is ignored for now, i.e., one group's advertising intervention does not change the other group's purchase outcomes.

Quantity Formula Point estimate
Attributed ROAS 500×50÷5,000 5
Incremental purchases at treatment scale 1,200−1,000 200
Incremental revenue return iROAS 200×50÷5,000 2
Incremental contribution after ad spend 200×50×40%−5,000 −1,000 dollars

Thus a 5x attributed ROAS can coexist with a loss. A “point estimate” is a single effect estimate obtained from this sample; these numbers are still subject to sampling variability, and whether there is sufficient statistical precision requires computing uncertainty with methods suited to the experimental unit and outcome distribution; you cannot declare significance from the table alone. If the two groups differ in size, first compare per-person outcomes, then specify the delivery scale to which you are extrapolating.

Users being randomly assigned to “eligible to see the ad” does not mean they actually saw it. First analyze the ITT (Intent To Treat, the treatment effect by original random assignment); do not retroactively filter treatment users who were actually exposed and compare them with all of control, or selection is reintroduced. Identifying the effect of actual exposure requires additional conditions and methods; identification cannot be accomplished merely by switching denominators in the absence of identifying assumptions.

Model experiments and channel incrementality experiments are not the same question

A model A/B experiment compares two delivery policies; a channel incrementality experiment compares advertising versus not advertising, reduced spend, or different budget levels. The former can prove B is better than A, but cannot on its own prove that the channel creates positive incremental profit relative to not spending. Geo experiments also have multiple intervention definitions; you must choose the question first, then the design. Google GeoX experiment types

Before launch, fix the primary metric, the minimum detectable effect (the smallest effect size the design can detect at a prespecified false-positive rate and statistical power; power is the probability of successfully detecting the effect when it exists), time span, label maturity period, guardrail metrics, and stopping rules. Shared budgets make experiment groups compete for the same quota; bidding lets one group affect the other's prices; cross-device and cross-region behavior contaminates group assignment. Even if user, geo, or time-block randomization is ultimately used, you must write down how these interferences are handled. Deciding whether to stop each time you check significance changes the actual false-positive rate.

In this example, the network cares about gross spread after media procurement, and the advertiser cares about incremental contribution. Both must be reported. Otherwise a policy can raise the network's short-term revenue by more aggressively pursuing conversions that are “easy to attribute but non-incremental,” and then treat customer churn as the sales team's problem.

Privacy and anti-fraud change observability

ATT (App Tracking Transparency) and Apple's attribution mechanisms are not synonyms. Apple's documentation states that using AdAttributionKit by itself does not require the ATT prompt; performing tracking as it defines it, or obtaining the advertising identifier, requires the corresponding permission. Privacy thresholds limit some postback fields, and certain fields may be empty; signatures and transaction IDs are used to counter forgery and replay. Apple Ad Attribution

The system implication is: the absence of a user-level postback cannot be automatically labeled “not converted,” aggregated postbacks cannot be arbitrarily split back into individuals, signed events do not prove incrementality, and missingness is not necessarily random. A new platform that subsidizes impression purchases has bought only delivery opportunities; it does not automatically obtain training labels that can be linked across customers and used under long-term authorization.

Fraud must be examined layer by layer. Whether the supply identity is real, whether the impression was actually delivered, whether the click was made by a human, whether the conversion is valid, whether attribution was hijacked, and whether a refund followed are different attack surfaces. ads.txt declares authorized sellers; sellers.json and SupplyChain improve transparency of sellers and the chain; these mechanisms alone cannot guarantee traffic quality or economic incrementality. IAB ads.txt, IAB supply chain FAQ

As in trading risk control, positive expectation per trade does not guarantee sustainable profitability. Correlated fake events, changes to attribution windows, chargebacks, and the publisher being paid first while the advertiser pays later can all make risk highly concentrated. An anti-fraud model is only one layer; you also need source-level risk limits, settlement reconciliation, contractual recourse, and gradual ramp-up.
