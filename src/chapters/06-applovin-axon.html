AppLovin and AXON

Product roles confirmable from public sources

According to public pages retrieved as of November 11, 2026, AppLovin describes Axon as an ad prediction engine that evaluates the value of impressions and bids according to advertisers' return goals. MAX provides app monetization and aggregation of multiple demand sources. MAX's public interface documentation describes a first-price auction, where real-time bidding can be combined with waterfall demand; its specific flow sends a win notice after a successful load and calls the billing URL after render. This sequence is MAX's interface semantics and must not be used in reverse to rewrite the definitions of all OpenRTB integrators. Axon official description, MAX auction flow

AppLovin's 2025 10-K discloses that advertising revenue is recognized net in an agent role, after deducting the cost of ad inventory, and distinguishes contracts based on impressions and actions. From this, you cannot derive advertisers' total spend or the platform-wide take rate directly from net revenue. The statement on the MAX website that it is free for publishers also does not mean there are no charges anywhere in the chain. Revenue recognition notes in AppLovin's 2025 10-K, MAX website FAQ

Data visibility and the right to use data for training must be kept separate

The company's Axon description lists the device, network, engagement, and advertiser-provided data and MAX win/loss notices that it claims to use, and states that the model does not ingest other bidders' bids or other bidders' user data. The 2025 10-K also discloses that Adjust data is by default not shared with AppLovin for use in the recommendation engine unless the customer directs it; the Apps business was sold in June 2025. These are the company's public statements or disclosures, not an independent audit of the data pipeline. Axon data description, AppLovin 2025 10-K

Thus, “MAX receives a bid in order to run the auction,” “Axon is permitted to use that bid,” “the training pipeline actually uses it,” and “only this company has it” are four different propositions. Owning a measurement company also does not automatically mean owning all of customers' data usable for training. Merging them into a single “data flywheel” writes the competitive advantage that needs to be proven into the conclusion in advance.

Public materials cannot determine Axon's current model architecture, parameter count, feature coverage, training frequency, loss function, or budget controller; nor can they separate the respective causal contributions of the model, creatives, supply, customer mix, and attribution rules to a given quarter's growth. The architecture diagrams and algorithms in this text are tools for understanding the problem, not a reverse-engineering report.

How a prediction advantage turns into retained profit

Here we use the comparison that, in single-agent decision-making, “the optimal value of additional information is nonnegative”. The premises are: competitors' strategies and the price distribution are fixed; the objective, available actions, and constraints such as budget are unchanged; obtaining and using the information has no extra cost; the new information is available at decision time and contains all the old information (the old signal can be recovered from the new one); conditional valuations are accurate and optimal decisions are permitted. Under these premises, the new decision-maker can at least ignore the added information and replicate the old policy, so the optimal expected return will not be worse. A rise in offline AUC proves neither the containment relationship between information sets nor that probability calibration and decisions are optimal, much less that all gains can be retained after market feedback.

Suppose a new model identifies more valuable opportunities. It can reduce low-value purchases, and it can also bid higher for high-value opportunities. The latter raises publisher revenue; competitors and publisher floor prices keep reacting, and customers may also demand better pricing. How rents are divided depends on whether the information is exclusive, whether access is stable, whether the model generalizes, whether customers have alternatives, and what fee structure the platform uses. Both “a model lead necessarily converts entirely into profit” and “auctions necessarily eliminate all advantage” are too strong.

When testing for a moat, the most informative comparison is not “whether the same kind of model can be built,” but whether, on the same supply surface and under comparable data permissions, maturity windows, and budget constraints, new advertisers and additional budget can generate sustained incremental contribution. The model, measurement, supply access, creatives, execution costs, and customer relationships must all enter the explanation together; selection bias must also be controlled, rather than showcasing only the surviving high-return customers.

Subsidies can buy opportunities for trial and error, but cannot directly buy unbiased labels. If labels come only from subsidized customers, low-priced traffic, or high-intent retargeting, the learned relationships may not transfer to the scaled market once full pricing begins. Early metrics worth verifying are customer retention after subsidies are removed, the quality of matured labels authorized for use, segment calibration, and the marginal profit curve—not event counts alone.

Three problems that attractive numbers easily obscure

In this paragraph, pCTR is the model's predicted click probability per impression; clearing CPM is the actual media payment converted to cost per thousand impressions; publisher eCPM is publisher revenue converted to an amount per thousand impressions. D7 ROAS in this paragraph is defined as cumulative attributed revenue for the same acquisition cohort over the first 7×24 hours from acquisition (a left-closed, right-open window) / that cohort's acquisition ad spend, using a matured window of a full 7 days; it is still an attributed return, not an incremental return.

If pCTR rises and clearing CPM rises in step while D7 ROAS stays unchanged, first decompose price and traffic mix; do not declare victory based on AUC. If MAX's publisher eCPM rises, it can show that sellers earn more revenue, but it does not automatically show that advertisers gained an equal amount of incremental profit. If subsidies bring in more advertisers and conversions, but retention falls once full pricing begins, the subsidy may be buying a training distribution that cannot be replicated.

For a would-be entrant, technical replicability is only one dimension of the problem. What truly requires evidence is: what labels you are permitted to obtain, what supply you can access, how customer incrementality is identified, under what constraints marginal returns remain positive, and how long these conditions can be sustained.
